# Deterministic loan matching

Finora compares reviewed public catalog records with the signed-in user's owned financial profile. It does not estimate lender approval, train a credit model, or substitute advertised “from” rates for a personal quote. The matching API is `POST /api/intelligence/catalog/matches`, protected by the Better Auth session guard. It accepts amount, term, optional category, savings, reserve months and stress percentage. The server loads income, essential expenses and existing monthly debt by the session user ID. Fin AI uses the same `matchPublicLoans` function; an external model can only select the schema-validated read-only tool and never sees matching results or profile values.

## Catalog quality

The catalog records lender, product, category/purposes, amount and term bounds, rate values/period/basis, repayment method, mandatory fee, insurance fee, free-text eligibility and early repayment terms, source URLs and review dates. `null` means unknown, including a missing insurance fee. A literal `0` means a sourced zero. The API classifies each product as `real` only when all ten tracked fields and a fixed rate are available; otherwise it is `incomplete`. Synthetic fixtures are marked `simulated`. “Real” means complete reviewed public data, not a binding lender offer.

The six currently reviewed public records are incomplete: no record publishes a verified repayment method and aggregate fee/insurance amount sufficient for a full product schedule. Finora therefore cannot mark any of these six as suitable today. This is a supported and expected `no_suitable_loan` result, not an empty or failed search. Source URLs and review dates remain visible for manual verification. The reviewed JSON is not automatically refreshed.

## Decision policy

`packages/financial-engine/src/matching.ts` is a pure function. It calls the existing `simulate` and `affordability` functions for all payment and budget figures. Identical inputs and catalog records produce identical output; equal status/score ties resolve by product ID. No database rows are written.

1. Check known amount, term and category conditions. A published free-text eligibility statement that cannot be checked from Finora's data remains `unknown`. The status is `does_not_meet_known_conditions` if any known condition fails, `insufficient_information` if none fails but one is unknown, and `meets_known_conditions` only when all are checked. These statuses never mean lender approval or rejection.
2. A schedule is computed only for a fixed published rate with known rate basis and compatible repayment method. An advertised starting rate or range alone is insufficient. The internal fixed due-date anchor affects no displayed figures and is not presented as a contractual date. Total including fees remains `null` if mandatory fee or insurance fee is unknown.
3. Baseline hard affordability requires the highest installment to fit the profile's monthly planning budget (default 35% income less existing debt, also capped by free cash after essential expenses and existing debt) and nonnegative residual cash. If the user requests a reserve and states savings, savings below essential expenses multiplied by reserve months is another hard failure. A failed hard check always gives `unsuitable`, regardless of weighted score.
4. The initial MCDA weights are monthly affordability **35**, total known borrowing cost **25**, stress resilience **20**, exact requested term fit **10**, and data transparency **10** percent. They are policy assumptions, configurable with `FINORA_MATCH_WEIGHTS`, `FINORA_MATCH_AFFORDABILITY_TARGET` and `FINORA_MATCH_STRESS_PERCENT`; they are not validated credit-risk parameters. Missing cost or schedule information scores zero in the relevant component. Cost is compared only among candidates without a known eligibility failure and with complete numeric totals. The default stress case reduces income by 20%; its residual cash flow is displayed and scored but is not a lender underwriting rule.

   Component scores are on 0–100 scales: monthly affordability is `100 × (1 − peak installment / monthly budget)` clamped to that range; known total cost is `100 × lowest comparable total / candidate total`; resilience is `100 × stressed residual / stressed income` clamped; term fit is 100 when both published bounds include the requested term, 25 when a bound is unknown, otherwise zero; transparency is the catalog's known-field count divided by ten. Unknown schedule or total cost gives zero for that component. The weighted sum is rounded to two decimals. These are comparative rules, not empirical probabilities.

5. Results sort `suitable`, then `insufficient_information`, then `unsuitable`, followed by weighted score descending and product ID ascending. This status order makes a high aggregate score unable to override a hard failure. Component scores, weights, hard reasons, missing terms and official sources accompany every entry. If no entry is suitable, the explicit outcome is `no_suitable_loan`.
6. When a known fixed-rate product breaches the baseline budget, the engine may calculate a lower amount or longer supported term whose highest installment fits the same budget. These are illustrations from published fixed terms, not lender availability or approval.

The UI reuses the existing Finora discovery filters and comparison overlay. It presents no personal score until amount and term are entered, marks provisional scores as provisional, and shows unknown costs as unknown. Fin AI renders the typed matching card. Because the external model never receives private matching output, the product-level explanation is deterministic, localized server/client text rather than an ungrounded model narrative.
