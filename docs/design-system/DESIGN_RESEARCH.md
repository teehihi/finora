# Design research: a loan ledger, not a loan storefront

**Reviewed:** 25 November 2026 UTC. **Question:** Can someone understand the consequences of a loan before creating an account or committing to it? The key design test is whether they can find the most demanding installment, understand which costs are included, and tell an estimate from a real obligation.

## Method and limits

Desk review of public, first-party surfaces: product examples and screenshots, help articles describing shipped interactions, a live Vietnamese calculator's exposed form/result structure, design-system guidance, and provider documentation. Links below lead to evidence; screenshots are linked rather than copied. Observations describe what these materials actually expose, not a logged-in walkthrough. No paid galleries, proprietary assets, tracked customer data, or recreated competitor screens. No latency measurements, native-device tests, or independent validation of vendors' marketing claims were part of this research. Dimensions not visible in a reference (such as transition timing, exact font family, or authenticated account state) are not inferred. All right-hand decisions below are **Finora proposals**, not claims about what already shipped in Finora.

## Three competing treatments to prototype

| Concept                        | Home and sign-in concept                                                                                                                                                             | Where it works / fails                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. The financial journal**   | A warm-ivory spread, generous opening sentence and discrete story sections. Sign-in pairs a large editorial brand statement with a compact email form.                               | Makes onboarding distinct; oversized headlines or decorative artwork can displace the next useful action at 390px. Reject if the CTA or first meaningful number is below the fold on a typical phone.                            |
| **B. The payment ledger**      | A restrained forest-ink focal amount, a dated line of context, a ruled list with aligned values. Authentication uses the same measured rhythm without looking like a banking portal. | Strongest through-line across discovery, simulator and saved plans; risks austerity if reduced to a spreadsheet. Bring in the approved mark, a warm canvas and one useful piece of human copy.                                   |
| **C. A conversation with Fin** | A relevant opening financial question, brief suggested prompts and structured answers. The same guide welcomes the user before email sign-in.                                        | Useful in the assistant or an empty state; as the global interaction model, routine tasks become slow and the app looks like a chatbot. A decorative mascot adds neither explanation nor identity when repeated on every screen. |

**Decision for design work:** use B for the core product, borrow A's opening typography and C only where there is a real question. Codename: **Sổ tay tài chính**. A signature detail is the ruled installment row: period at the leading edge, the total aligned to the trailing edge, a quieter line for principal and interest when expanded. It should hold up in black and white. Brand warmth comes from original Finora assets, not artificial gold, stock portraits, gloss or generic “AI” gradients. This desk recommendation needs rendered evaluation: it is not evidence the prototypes or final UI passed.

## What to ask in the visual review

- **At 390px**: can someone read a real or explicitly absent result, know the next action, and edit an assumption without scrolling past a poster? At 360px and enlarged text, a long Vietnamese currency amount must wrap or reflow without slicing the currency or clipping accents.
- **At 1440px**: is the extra space doing useful work? On Simulator, inputs and results should be visible together; in chat, a substantive plan can sit alongside prose; on sign-in the form stays at readable width. A second column does not justify fake content.
- **For keyboard, touch and reduced motion**: selected chart data also has text; no swipe-only action; focus reaches all filters and returns after dismissing sheets; errors are announced near the field. Time-consuming animations are not a substitute for immediate pending/success feedback.
- **For financial honesty**: projected money, a saved scenario and an active obligation should never be interchangeable. Unknown fees are not free fees. Missing profile data is not zero income. A mock assistant is clearly identified. No “approved” badge, credit score, countdown to borrow or unsourced superlative.

These are acceptance questions for Finora, not usability-test outcomes.
